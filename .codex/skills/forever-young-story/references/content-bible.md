# Forever Young Story — content bible (draft)

## Channel promise

Growing older can mean becoming freer, wiser, healthier, and more interesting.
Each clip should leave the viewer with one useful idea and one warmer feeling
about their own life.

## Audience

- Primary: Thai adults about 40–65.
- Secondary: younger viewers interested in longevity, family stories, and
  lessons that remain useful across generations.
- Speak to capable adults. Never use patronizing language such as “คนแก่” or
  imply that youth is the only desirable state.

## Content pillars

1. **Live Well** — sleep, movement, food, stress, brain and heart health.
2. **Life Stories** — real people who began again, changed careers, learned,
   loved, or created meaningful work later in life.
3. **Then & Now** — warm nostalgia from the 1980s–2000s connected to a useful
   present-day insight, not nostalgia for its own sake.
4. **Young at Heart** — relationships, confidence, play, curiosity, purpose,
   friendship, and emotional resilience.
5. **Longer Future** — credible longevity research and technology without
   miracle claims, supplement hype, or science-fiction certainty.

## Editorial test

Accept a topic only when it provides at least one of: practical usefulness,
emotional recognition, a surprising evidence-based insight, or a memorable
human story. Reject generic quotes, copied motivational lists, miracle cures,
celebrity gossip without meaning, and fear-based medical clickbait.

## Story structure

For a normal 55–75 second clip, use 9–12 scenes including the outro:

1. A truthful question, recognizable moment, or surprising contrast.
2. Why this matters in ordinary life.
3. Two to four specific facts, actions, or story turns.
4. A realistic takeaway the viewer can try or remember.
5. A warm closing thought and one conversational question.
6. One dedicated channel outro.

Change the visual idea every 4–7 seconds. Add scenes only for a new fact,
emotion, comparison, or action; never pad the runtime.

## Thai writing voice

Sound like a warm, observant friend with life experience: relaxed, quietly
playful, clear, and respectful. Prefer concrete scenes and everyday language.
Use humor gently; never mock bodies, memory, illness, money, relationships, or
age. Avoid announcer phrasing, lectures, excessive exclamation marks, and long
dramatic pauses between scenes.

Draft TTS direction:

> Read aloud in a warm, natural, conversational Thai voice. Sound mature,
> reassuring, quietly playful, and emotionally present, like a thoughtful
> friend sharing a meaningful story over coffee. Keep the flow connected and
> the pace medium. Use gentle changes in pitch and rhythm, brief pauses, and a
> subtle smile. Never sound like a news anchor, advertisement, medical expert,
> stage performer, or motivational speaker.

## Health and safety

- Distinguish general education from personal medical advice.
- Do not promise prevention, cure, reversal of aging, or guaranteed lifespan.
- Give quantities or recommendations only when supported by an authoritative
  source and appropriate context.
- For symptoms, medicines, supplements, chronic illness, or major lifestyle
  changes, invite consultation with a qualified professional without using a
  frightening disclaimer.
- Do not use before/after bodies to shame viewers.
