# Forever Young Story — visual bible (draft)

## Visual identity

Target style: **premium warm cinematic editorial storytelling**. The channel
should feel modern, optimistic, emotionally real, and quietly luxurious—not a
clinic, retirement advertisement, beauty campaign, or generic stock montage.

## People

- Portray Thai and Asian adults roughly 40–70 as energetic, attractive,
  expressive human beings with authentic skin texture, smile lines, gray hair,
  and individual style.
- Show agency: walking, creating, learning, dancing, cooking, working,
  traveling, laughing, resting, and connecting with others.
- Avoid artificial age regression, porcelain skin, frailty stereotypes,
  infantilization, and overly glamorous beauty-retouching.
- No fixed presenter is canonical during draft review. If a presenter is later
  approved, preserve face and identity while adapting wardrobe and emotion.

## Color and light

- Core palette: sunrise gold `#E8A84C`, warm cream `#F4E8D2`, deep teal
  `#173F43`, sage `#809777`, and plum `#613E57`.
- Favor sunrise, window light, golden hour, warm practical lamps, and soft
  reflected fill. Preserve natural skin tones.
- Use controlled contrast, subtle film grain, material texture, and gentle
  depth. Avoid neon cyberpunk, hospital white, gray sadness, and oversaturated
  orange skin.

## Composition

- One emotional idea and one dominant subject per frame.
- Use foreground, midground, and background to tell a visible story.
- Fill roughly 70–80% of the frame with meaningful visual information while
  keeping a calm lower-center subtitle band.
- Scene 01 must function as key art: a recognizable human moment, the topic,
  and a short 2–5 word hook. Typography is large, clean, elegant, and readable
  on a phone.
- Scene 02 onward contains no embedded text, subtitles, labels, watermark, or
  logo unless the story requires a real-world artifact.

## Reject list

Reject and regenerate: generic smiling senior stock photos; white-background
wellness ads; fake doctors; pill or supplement hero shots; miracle before/after
imagery; lonely bench clichés; excessive soft-focus; plastic skin; deformed
hands; mismatched ages; unreadable text; pasted people; or scenes that do not
show the narration's specific action or emotion.
